# Option X - Cross-Examination Debate

Use `X` when a claim or position must survive a real adversarial test.

## Best For

- incompatible positions,
- burden-of-proof conflicts,
- stress-testing a strong claim before the swarm relies on it,
- cases where tidy synthesis would blur a real disagreement.

## Output Shape

- Moderator reasoning
- exact proposition under test
- explicit affirmative and negative positions
- opening case for each side
- cross-examination
- rebuttal / strongest challenge response
- adjudicator verdict with burden-of-proof outcome
- route update
- next option selection

## Quality Bar

- the proposition is crisp enough to attack directly,
- both sides engage the strongest challenge rather than restating preferences,
- burden of proof is visible in the verdict,
- the round changes route confidence rather than merely sounding sharp,
- the outcome says whether the proposition survived, failed, or remains unresolved.

## Structured Form

Prefer this staged shape:
1. define the proposition and the two positions,
2. let each side present its opening case,
3. run one real cross-examination pass,
4. require each side to answer the strongest challenge,
5. issue a verdict that states whether the proposition survived the adversarial test.

Use `D` instead of `X` when the real need is route selection rather than adversarial claim testing.

## Failure Modes

- debating vague or movable propositions,
- mistaking style for burden-of-proof success,
- letting both sides survive without a verdict,
- performing courtroom theater with no route consequence,
- using `X` when ordinary adjudication would have been enough.
